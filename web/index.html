how it works

radio clocks lock onto a longwave carrier (DCF77 77.5 kHz, WWVB/JJY60/MSF 60 kHz, BPC 68.5 kHz, JJY40 40 kHz). no sound card outputs that directly. trick: emit a square tone at carrier / k (default k=4) at low amplitude. the phone speaker and amp are nonlinear, so they generate the k-th harmonic, which lands exactly on the carrier. that tone is amplitude-keyed each second to send the time frame in the station protocol.

setup: max system volume, plug in headphones or a 3.5 mm cable (the wire is the antenna), put the watch in manual receive, lay it within a few cm of the jack or speaker. give it 3 to 10 minutes. reposition the watch if it will not lock, the sweet spot is fussy. keep the room quiet and wifi/video off nearby.

if a band will not lock, try a different k: each phone radiates a different harmonic best. the spectrum view shows the fundamental you are emitting; the harmonic that does the work is inaudible and off-screen.

on iPhone: the physical mute switch silences Web Audio, so flip it off. iOS speakers also roll off hard near 19 kHz, which is the DCF77 k=4 fundamental, so on iPhone pick k=5 (15.5 kHz) for DCF77.

which watch, which station

radio controlled watches and clocks all listen on one of six longwave transmitters. the brand does not matter, the region the watch was tuned for does. pick the station for that region:

regionstationcarrier
continental EuropeDCF7777.5 kHz
UK and IrelandMSF60 kHz
North AmericaWWVB60 kHz
Japan (west, south)JJY6060 kHz
Japan (north, east)JJY4040 kHz
ChinaBPC68.5 kHz

Casio is the big one. Wave Ceptor, G-Shock, Oceanus, Edifice, Pro Trek, Lineage. anything marked Multi Band 6 (GW-M5610, GW-5000, GWX, Oceanus OCW) receives all six transmitters above, so any station here can sync it. the older Multi Band 5 and Multi Band 3 drop BPC and sometimes MSF. Casio receives through a ferrite bar near the 9 o clock side of the case, so lay that edge against the speaker.

Citizen radio controlled and Eco-Drive Radio Controlled (Promaster, Attesa, Skyhawk, Perpetual Chrono) cover DCF77, WWVB and JJY, with worldwide models adding MSF and BPC. Junghans (Mega, Max Bill, Force) is German and DCF77 first, worldwide variants add WWVB and JJY. Seiko, Braun desk clocks, Oregon Scientific, La Crosse, Bürk and Lorus all follow the same six band scheme by region.

auto-receive watches choose the band from their home city setting. if yours will not pick the band you are emitting, temporarily set the home city to one in that region (Frankfurt for DCF77, London for MSF, New York or Denver for WWVB, Tokyo for JJY, Beijing for BPC), force a manual receive, then set the city back afterward.

the author of the original app reports a Casio 5610 locking with the case 9 o clock edge on the speaker. expect a few minutes. watches verify a full minute frame plus parity before they accept the time, so a clean uninterrupted run matters more than raw volume.

runs fully offline once loaded. nothing leaves the device except the optional clock calibration.
